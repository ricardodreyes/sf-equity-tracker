# Can we publish the analysis the City owes? Partly.

## What Sec. 124.2(a) requires
Per **Neighborhood** (the 41 ACS/Analysis Neighborhoods): unsheltered persons, that neighborhood's
share of citywide unsheltered persons, shelter + transitional beds, and share of citywide beds.
The first analysis is explicitly keyed to the **2024 PIT Count**.

## What is actually published
- **Beds:** we have them per facility, but only for the 30 sites in HSH's 4/13/2026 Temporary Shelter
  Programs report = **3,020 mapped beds against a citywide HIC figure of 5,148**. We are missing ~41%,
  mostly transitional housing programs with no published site list.
- **Unsheltered:** the 2024 PIT Count Report (doc 31917, Figure 02) publishes unsheltered counts
  **by supervisorial district only**, plus a "Confidential/Scattered Site" line of 203. There is no
  published neighborhood-level unsheltered table, and no PIT dataset on DataSF at all.

## Consequence
The statutory test **cannot be computed by anyone from published data**, including by the public.
The City can compute it because it holds the ONE System data; we cannot. That is the strongest
argument for why the report has to be published, and it is a finding in its own right.

## Two cautions that must reach the site, loudly
1. **Geography changes the answer.** The Tenderloin Analysis Neighborhood holds 870 beds (28.8% of
   mapped beds) but spans D3, D5 and D6. Run at district level, D5 reads as under-served; run at
   neighborhood level, the Tenderloin is the most saturated place in the city. The statute uses
   Neighborhood. A district-level fair-share result is a **proxy**, and must be labelled one.
2. **Our bed denominator is incomplete** (3,020 of 5,148). Shares are therefore approximate. Publish
   the gap next to the number rather than burying it.

## Verdict
Ship the district-level proxy clearly labelled as a proxy, publish the neighborhood bed table
(which we do have), and state plainly that the unsheltered side at neighborhood level is unpublished.
"We built as much of your report as public data allows; here is the piece only you can supply" is a
stronger and more honest position than pretending to have reproduced the statutory analysis.
